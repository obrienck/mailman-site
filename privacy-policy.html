Mailman Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 2, 2026

Mailman ("the app") is a mobile email client developed and published by an individual developer ("we," "us," or "the developer"), not a company. This policy explains what data Mailman accesses, how it's used, and how it's stored.

The short version: Mailman has no backend server. Everything the app does happens directly between your device, your email provider, and (only if you choose to use AI features) the AI provider you personally configure. We never see, collect, or have access to your email or its contents.

1. What data Mailman accesses

When you connect an email account to Mailman, you sign in directly with your email provider (e.g. Google) using that provider's own official sign-in flow. Mailman never sees or stores your password. Depending on the provider, Mailman requests permission to:

  • Read your messages, labels, and folders, so they can be displayed in the app.
  • Compose and send messages on your behalf, so you can reply and write new mail from the app.
  • Move messages to Trash, so you can delete mail from the app (for Gmail, this uses Gmail's own Trash — it is not a permanent, unrecoverable delete, and follows Gmail's own retention rules).
  • Read your basic profile information (name and email address), so the app can identify which account is connected.

For Gmail specifically, Mailman requests the gmail.modify scope (read, compose, send, and move-to-trash) and basic openid/email profile scopes. Mailman does not request permission to permanently and irrevocably delete mail, bypassing Trash.

2. Where your data is stored

All of your mail — message content, metadata, and attachments you open — is stored only on your own device, in the app's local database and file storage. Mailman does not operate a server, and your mail is never copied to, or stored on, any infrastructure the developer controls.

Your email provider's authentication tokens are stored in your device's secure OS-level keychain (Android Keystore / iOS Keychain), the same secure storage mechanism used by banking and password-manager apps. They are never transmitted anywhere except directly to your email provider, to keep your session signed in.

3. AI features: what happens when you use them

Mailman lets you optionally configure an AI provider of your choosing to summarize email threads and suggest to-do items. This is entirely opt-in and entirely under your control:

  • If you don't configure an AI provider, no email content is ever sent anywhere for AI processing.
  • If you configure a local AI provider (e.g. Ollama or LM Studio running on your own computer), the content needed for that feature (e.g. a thread's messages) is sent directly from your device to your own computer over your local network. It never leaves your network.
  • If you configure a hosted AI provider (e.g. Anthropic, OpenAI, xAI, or another API you connect using your own API key), the content needed for that feature is sent directly from your device to that provider's API, using your own credentials. That provider's own privacy policy and data-handling practices govern what happens to that data once it reaches them — Mailman has no visibility into or control over that.

In every case, this data is sent directly from your device to the destination you configured. It is never routed through, or seen by, any server operated by Mailman's developer, because no such server exists.

4. What Mailman does not do

  • Mailman does not have a backend server, and does not collect, transmit, or store your mail anywhere except on your own device.
  • Mailman does not sell your data, or any data, to anyone. There is nothing to sell — we don't have it.
  • Mailman does not show ads and does not contain advertising or ad-tracking SDKs.
  • Mailman does not use analytics or usage-tracking services.
  • Mailman does not share your data with third parties, except as described in Section 3 (only when you explicitly configure and use an AI feature, sent only to the provider you chose).

5. Data retention and deletion

Because all data lives only on your device:

  • Disconnecting an account inside Mailman removes that account's locally stored mail and credentials from your device.
  • Uninstalling Mailman removes all of its locally stored data, including cached mail and credentials, following your device operating system's normal app-uninstall behavior.
  • Revoking Mailman's access from your email provider's own account security settings (e.g. Google Account → Security → Third-party access) immediately invalidates Mailman's ability to access that account, from the provider's side.

6. Children's privacy

Mailman is not directed at, or intended for use by, children, and we do not knowingly collect data from children. Since Mailman has no backend server and collects no data of its own in the first place, there is no data of any user — child or otherwise — held by the developer.

7. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the "Last updated" date above will be updated accordingly. Since Mailman has no user accounts or contact mechanism of its own, continued use of the app after a change constitutes acceptance of the updated policy.
